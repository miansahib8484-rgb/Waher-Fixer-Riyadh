Washing Machine Not Spinning: Common Causes and Fixes

You open the door after a wash and the clothes come out dripping. The drum filled and turned, but it never spun properly. It is one of the most common washing machine problems, and the cause is often simpler than it looks. Here is how to work out what is going on before you call anyone.

Why won't the drum spin?

  • Overloaded or unbalanced load: many machines stop spinning when the laundry bunches up on one side.
  • Water not drained: some machines won't start the spin until the water is out, so the fault may be the filter or the pump.
  • Drive belt: it may be loose or broken, so the motor runs but the drum stays still.
  • Motor or carbon brushes: on brushed motors, worn brushes weaken or stop the rotation.
  • Door lock or lid switch: the machine won't run if the system can't confirm the door is closed.
  • Control board or sensors: they control the spin stage, and a fault stops it.

What you can check safely

  • Stop the machine, redistribute the laundry and remove part of the load if it is heavy.
  • Make sure the door is fully closed and no clothing is blocking the latch.
  • Check the pump filter and drain hose after unplugging the machine; water left in the drum prevents spinning.
  • Run the spin program on its own to see whether the drum turns.

Don't open the machine's cover or touch electrical parts yourself.

Error codes linked to spin problems

On many Samsung and LG machines the code UE shows up when the load is unbalanced. Other codes point to the motor, the door or drainage. Codes vary by model, so check the manual and tell the technician the code when you call.

How the technician diagnoses a machine that will not spin

  • Check the load balance and the balance or load sensor.
  • Confirm the water drains; a blocked drain pump or filter can prevent the spin.
  • Test the door lock or lid switch.
  • Inspect the drive belt and pulleys, and test the motor and carbon brushes if fitted.
  • Check the control board and its connections.
