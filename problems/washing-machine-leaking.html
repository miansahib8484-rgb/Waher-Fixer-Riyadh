Washing Machine Leaking Water: Find the Source and Fix It

A wet patch next to the washing machine is never welcome, but where the water comes from tells you a lot. The door, a hose, the filter cap and the drawer each leave different clues. First stop the damage, then find the source.

What to do as soon as you notice a leak

  • Stop the machine and unplug it.
  • Turn off the water tap feeding it.
  • Dry the area to avoid slipping and floor damage.
  • Work out where the water is coming from before you call.

Where the leak comes from and the likely cause

  • From the door (front-load): the rubber door seal is worn or clogged with lint, or the door is not closing properly.
  • From the back: inlet or drain hoses or their connections are loose or cracked.
  • From the front, low down: the pump filter cover is not tight.
  • From the detergent drawer: excess foam or a blocked drawer.
  • From underneath, with noise during the spin: possibly a worn tub seal or bearing.

Too much foam

Too much detergent, or soap not made for automatic machines, makes foam that escapes from the drawer or door and looks like a leak. Use the right detergent in the recommended amount.

How we find the source of a leak

  • Dry the machine and run a short cycle to watch where the water appears.
  • Inspect the door seal, the inlet and drain hoses, the filter cap and the detergent drawer.
  • Test the inlet valve, the tub seal and the bearing.
  • Check that the hose connections are tight.

When do you need a technician?

If the leak keeps coming back after you have tightened the connections and the filter cap, or the water seems to come from underneath the machine, stop using it and call us. A technician will inspect it at your home in Riyadh, show you what failed and what it will cost before any work starts, and the repair is covered by a 30-day warranty on parts and labor.
