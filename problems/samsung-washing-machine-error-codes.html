Samsung Washing Machine Error Codes and What They Mean

A code on the display tells you where to start looking. Here are the Samsung codes most people see, what they usually point to, and which checks are safe to do yourself.

Why the same code can mean different things

Samsung codes are short: a letter and a number, or two letters. On some models the same code has a slightly different meaning, and older machines may use codes that are not listed here. Your manual has the exact list for your model, so check it first.

The table below covers codes that are widely documented. Use it as a starting point, not a final answer. If you can, take a photo of the display and of the model label before you call.

Common Samsung error codes at a glance

Many codes appear in pairs because newer and older displays write them differently.

What to do first when a code appears

  • Switch the machine off, then unplug it.
  • Close the water tap if the code is about water, a leak or drainage.
  • Look for simple causes: a bent hose, a door not shut, a sock wedged in the seal, a full filter.
  • Wait a few minutes, plug in, and run a short cycle to see whether the code returns.
  • Do not open the back or top panels, and do not force the door.

Water in, water out: 4E, 4C, 5E and SE

A 4E or 4C code usually means the machine waited for water and did not get enough. A closed tap or a bent hose is the common reason. The small mesh filter where the hose meets the machine can also clog with scale. Unplug the machine and close the tap before you take the hose off to look.

5E or SE is the opposite: the water is not leaving. Our guide to a washing machine that is not draining walks through the hose and filter checks. If the pump is silent or humming and nothing drains, stop there and call.

Unbalanced load and door codes: UE, UB, dE and dC

UE or UB is the code most people can fix alone. Open the door, spread the clothes out, and avoid washing a single heavy item such as a blanket. Check that the machine stands level on its feet.

A door code usually means the latch was not registered as closed. Make sure no sleeve is caught in the seal, then close the door firmly. If the code stays, the lock assembly or its wiring may be the cause. That needs a technician. Please do not try to bypass the lock.

Leak, heater and motor codes: LE, LC, HE, HC, 1E and 3E

For these, we suggest you do less, not more. LE or LC usually means water was found where it should not be. Close the tap, unplug the machine and look for wet hoses or connections. Our page on a leaking washing machine lists the usual places.

HE or HC points to the heating circuit, and 1E to the water level sensor. 3E often relates to the motor or drive. For all three, switching off and restarting once is the only home step worth taking.

What if the code comes back after a reset?

Switching off and on clears a one-time glitch. A power cut or a half-closed door can trigger a code that never returns. If the same code shows again in the next cycle, treat it as real.

Repeated resets will not fix a worn part. Running the machine with a leak or a heating fault can also make the damage bigger. Before you call, note these:

  • The code and the stage when it appears: filling, washing, draining or spinning.
  • The model number from the label.
  • What you have already checked.
