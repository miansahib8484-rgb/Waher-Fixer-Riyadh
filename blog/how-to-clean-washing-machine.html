How to Clean a Washing Machine and Keep It Working Well

A washing machine cleans your clothes, but nobody cleans the washing machine. A few minutes now and then stops most smells and blocked filters. Here is a simple routine you can do yourself, without opening anything up.

Steps to clean the washing machine inside

  • Drum: run the drum-clean program if there is one, or an empty hot cycle with a washing machine cleaner, following your manual.
  • Door seal (front-load): wipe it with a damp cloth and dry it, paying attention to the folds where lint collects.
  • Detergent drawer: pull it out and wash it with water to remove detergent residue, then clean its housing.
  • Pump filter: unplug the machine, prepare a bowl and a towel, then take the filter out, clean it and refit it tightly.
  • Hoses: check them for cracks and leaks.

A simple maintenance schedule

  • After every wash: leave the door and drawer slightly open so the machine dries.
  • Every week: wipe the door seal.
  • Every month or two, depending on use: clean the drum, drawer and filter.
  • Whenever you notice a smell or slow draining: clean the filter straight away.

These are general recommendations; check your machine's manual for what the manufacturer advises.

Common mistakes that shorten a machine's life

  • Using more detergent than needed, which builds up residue and foam.
  • Overloading the machine.
  • Leaving wet laundry inside for long periods.
  • Forgetting to check pockets for coins and clips.

When cleaning is not enough

If the smell stays after a proper clean, water remains in the drum or a leak shows up, cleaning won't fix it.
