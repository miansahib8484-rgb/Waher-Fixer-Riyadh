Clothes Coming Out of the Washing Machine Wet or Soapy: Causes and Fixes

You open the door and the clothes are dripping, or they feel slimy and smell of soap. Both problems are common, and both have simple causes more often than people expect. In this guide we explain why clothes come out of the washing machine wet or soapy, give you a quick way to tell which problem you have, and show which fixes you can do yourself and which need a technician.

Wet or soapy: how can you tell which one it is?

Start with one test. Run a normal cycle with a small, balanced load, then look at the clothes and at the drum. The table below points you to the likely cause.

What you noticeMost likely cause
Water in the drum at the endDrain problem: blocked filter, hose or pump
No water, but clothes are drippingWeak spin: load, balance, setting or motor side
Clothes bunched on one sideUnbalanced or overloaded drum
Foam in the drum or white marks on dark clothesToo much detergent or hard water residue
Clothes feel slimy and smell of soapPoor rinse: low water, short cycle or too much detergent

Why do clothes come out wet?

Wet clothes mean the final spin did not remove enough water. That has a few possible causes. Some are about how you load the machine, and some are faults.

  • Unbalanced or overloaded drum. A single heavy item, like a blanket or a towel, can bunch up. The machine senses the imbalance and slows the spin to protect itself. Many machines also stop and try again.
  • Spin speed setting. A low spin setting or a delicate program leaves more water in the clothes by design.
  • Drain problem. If the pump or the drain filter is blocked, the water does not leave fully, and the spin cannot work well.
  • Worn belt. On belt-driven machines, a loose or worn belt can make the drum spin weakly.
  • Motor or carbon brushes. On some models, worn carbon brushes or a motor fault reduce the spin speed.
  • Sensor or control fault. If the machine cannot read the drum speed or the water level properly, it may cut the spin short.

Quick checks you can do for wet clothes

Try these in order. They take a few minutes and cost nothing. Always unplug the machine and close the tap before you touch any part inside.

First, wash a smaller load and mix large and small items. Do not wash one heavy item alone, because it will bunch up. Second, choose a normal program with a higher spin setting. Third, check the drain filter: unplug the machine, place a tray and towels on the floor, open the small cover at the bottom, and clean the filter slowly. Many people find coins, lint or hair there. If you want to know more, see our page on a washing machine that is not spinning.

When wet clothes mean a real fault

If a small balanced load still comes out dripping, and the drain filter is clean, the cause is probably inside. A burning smell, a loud noise during the spin, or a drum that turns freely by hand when the machine is off can point to a belt or motor side problem. We say probably, because the same signs can have different causes on different models.

Do not open the machine yourself. A technician can test the drain pump, the belt, the motor and the sensor, and then tell you which part is at fault before any repair. Noise during the spin is covered in our guide on noise and vibration.

Why do clothes come out soapy?

Soapy clothes mean detergent was not rinsed out. It is rarely a broken part. More often it is a setting or a habit.

  • Too much detergent. This is the most common cause. More detergent does not clean better. It makes more foam and leaves residue.
  • Wrong detergent type. Front-load machines use less water, so they need low-foam detergent, often marked HE. Regular detergent can foam too much.
  • Blocked detergent dispenser. If the drawer is clogged with old detergent, it may release a thick lump instead of a smooth flow.
  • Low water level. A small load can make the machine use little water, so there is less to rinse with.
  • Short or quick cycle. These programs rinse less. They are fine for lightly dirty clothes, but not for heavy loads.
  • Hard water. Minerals in the water can react with detergent and leave a white film. Riyadh homes often have hard water, so it is worth checking. See our guide on hard water and washing machines.

How to fix soapy clothes

Reduce the detergent first. Use the amount on the pack for your load size, and use even less for a small load. Check that you are using the right type for a front-load machine.

Then run an extra rinse, or a rinse-and-spin program, to clear what is left in the clothes. Pull out the detergent drawer, wash it in warm water and clean the slot behind it with a soft brush.

Finally, run an empty hot cycle with a cleaner made for washing machines, or follow our steps for cleaning your washing machine. Residue builds up in the drum, the seal and the filter, and it can keep coming back onto clothes until it is cleaned.

When should you call a technician?

Call if clothes stay wet after you have tried the quick checks, if water is left in the drum, if the machine shuts off before the spin, or if you notice a smell of burning. Also call if soapy clothes continue after you reduced the detergent, ran extra rinses and cleaned the drawer, because the water inlet or the level sensor may need checking.
