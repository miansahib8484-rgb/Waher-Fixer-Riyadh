Repair Guide & Common Faults

Dryer repair and dryer service at your home

We offer dryer repair and dryer service in Riyadh. The dryer technician checks the heating element, thermostat, lint filter and air path at your home, then tells you the fault and the cost before any work begins. Every repair has a 30-day warranty on parts and labor.

Common dryer faults

The faults we see most are a dryer that doesn't heat, clothes that stay damp after the cycle, a drum that won't turn, or a machine that is noisy or runs too hot. Usually it is a blocked lint filter or air duct, a failed heating element or thermostat, or a broken drum belt. We inspect first and find the fault before repairing anything.

The danger of lint build-up

Lint doesn't only slow drying. Built up in the filter and exhaust duct, it raises the temperature inside the machine and can become a fire risk. Clean the lint filter after every cycle and make sure the exhaust duct isn't blocked or kinked.

The dryer runs but clothes stay damp

That usually points to restricted airflow, a blocked filter or an overloaded drum, though a weak heating element or sensor is possible too. Clean the filter and reduce the load first. If it doesn't improve, have the machine inspected.

Tips to extend the life of your dryer

Clean the lint filter after every use and don't overload the dryer. If yours is a condenser type, empty the water tank after every cycle. Never dry clothes soaked in oil or flammable substances, and keep the air path clear.

Washer-dryer combo repair

Some machines combine washing and drying in one drum, so they can suffer washing faults such as drainage, spinning and leaks, and dryer faults such as no heat and blocked airflow. We inspect the machine to find which side has failed before repairing it.

How a dryer works and which parts fail

A dryer draws in air and warms it with a heating element, controlled by a thermostat and protected by a thermal fuse. The motor turns the drum through a belt and rollers. The damp air then passes through the lint filter to the exhaust duct (or to the condenser, in condenser dryers), while a moisture sensor tracks how dry the clothes are. So faults come from a blocked filter or duct, a failed heating element or thermostat, a broken belt or worn rollers, or a faulty sensor.

What to prepare before the technician arrives

Note the brand and model, clean the lint filter before the visit if you can, and tell us whether the problem is no heat, slow drying, a drum that won't turn, or noise. If it is a condenser dryer, empty the water tank, and tell us if you noticed a burning smell or unusual heat.
