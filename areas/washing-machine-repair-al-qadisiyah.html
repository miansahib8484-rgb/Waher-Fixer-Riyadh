Quick safety rule for all three faults

Before you touch anything, unplug the machine and close the water tap. Never open electrical parts and never try to force a locked door. If you are unsure, stop and message us.

The machine will not drain

Water stays in the drum after the cycle, and the clothes come out soaked. Our area guide groups Al Qadisiyah with Al Rimal, and the first checks are the same in both. Full steps are in the not draining guide.

  • Check that the drain hose is not bent or squashed behind the machine.
  • Look at the drain pump filter, if your model has an access panel. Keep a towel and a shallow tray ready, because water will come out.
  • Remove small items that may have been caught, such as coins or socks.
  • If the water still stays, stop. A blocked or failed pump needs a technician.

The machine will not spin

The drum fills and turns slowly, but the final fast spin never happens, or the cycle stops with wet clothes. The cause is often simple and often not.

  • Open the door and see whether the load is heavy, uneven or tangled in one lump. Redistribute it and run a spin cycle alone.
  • Make sure the machine is level and does not rock when you push the top corners.
  • If there is water left inside, the machine may be waiting to drain first, so see the previous section.
  • If it still will not spin, a worn belt, a motor issue or a board fault is possible. Read the not spinning guide or call us.

The machine leaks

Find where the water comes from before you do anything else. Dry the floor, run a short cycle, and watch.

How does the technician diagnose a drain fault?

The technician checks the drain hose and the filter first, then listens for the pump when the machine tries to empty. A pump that hums but moves no water is a different story from one that makes no sound at all. The first often means a blockage. The second can point to the pump or the board. You hear which it is before any work starts.

How does the technician diagnose a spin fault?

Most of the time the question is whether the machine is refusing to spin or is being stopped on purpose. A load out of balance, water still in the drum or a lid or door that is not registering can all stop the spin. Only after those are ruled out does the search move to the belt, the motor and the control board.

How does the technician diagnose a leak?

Water is dried from the floor, a short cycle is run and the technician watches where it first appears. That one observation usually separates a door seal problem from a hose problem and from a leak inside. It also saves you from paying attention to the wrong part.

Which of these can I describe best before the visit?

Give us the moment it goes wrong, the place the water shows, and any sound you heard. Three short sentences are enough.
