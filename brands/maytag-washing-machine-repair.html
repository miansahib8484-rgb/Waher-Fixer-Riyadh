About Maytag washers

Maytag is an American brand, and it is part of Whirlpool Corporation. Many Maytag washers are large-capacity machines, both top-loaders and front-loaders, built for heavy family loads. That is useful in a big household, but it also means the drum, the drive and the suspension work hard.

If you own a Whirlpool, this page may help you too. On many models the two brands use similar designs and similar parts, so the common faults overlap. Tell us the exact model and we will confirm what applies.

Top-loaders: agitator, drive coupling and transmission

On some top-loading Maytag models, the clothes are moved by a central agitator driven through a coupling or a transmission under the tub. When that part wears, you may hear the motor running but see little or no movement in the drum. Another sign is a clunking or grinding sound at the start of the wash.

A worn coupling is often a smaller repair than a damaged transmission. The technician can tell the difference after opening the machine at your home, and the technician will say so before starting.

Lid switch, drain pump and inlet valve

Three small parts cause a lot of stops. The lid switch tells the machine the lid is closed. If it wears out, the machine may not fill, agitate or spin. The drain pump pushes water out, and a blocked or failing pump leaves water in the tub. The inlet valve lets water in, and a weak valve can mean a slow fill or no fill.

For a washer that stops with water inside, our page on a washing machine that will not spin lists what to look at. Please never bypass the lid switch. It is a safety part.

Control board, spin faults and leaks

Large washers carry a lot of electronics. A control board fault can show as dead buttons, a cycle that restarts, or a machine that does nothing at all. Water inside the board area is a bad sign, so wipe up any leak and keep the machine unplugged.

Spin problems on a heavy load often come from an unbalanced drum, a worn belt or motor part, or a lid or door switch. For water on the floor, start with washing machine leaking. Common sources are the hoses, the door seal on front-loaders, and the pump.

Looking after a large-capacity washer

A big drum is tempting. People pack it full, and then the load bunches up and strains the drive. On many models, loading to the manufacturer's limit and spreading heavy items such as towels and bedding evenly helps the washer last longer. Check your manual for the limit.

Keep the machine level. Use a spirit level if you can, and adjust the feet. A washer that rocks on the floor wears its suspension and bearings faster. Also check that the hoses are not kinked behind the machine. A pinched drain hose can look like a pump fault, and fixing it costs nothing.
