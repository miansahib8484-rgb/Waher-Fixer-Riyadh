A short routine that prevents many Beko faults

Many Beko washers are front-loaders with a drain filter at the bottom front. If you clear it from time to time, you avoid some of the most common stops. Unplug the machine, close the tap, lay a towel and a shallow tray under the cover, and open the filter slowly. Water will come out.

Run an empty hot cycle now and then, and leave the door ajar after washing. Our guide on cleaning a washing machine has the steps. Nothing here replaces a technician when the machine truly fails.

Drain pump and filter problems

If the washer stops with water inside, or the clothes come out soaked, the filter or the pump is a likely place to start. Small items such as coins, buttons and bra wires get into the pump and block it. A pump can also hum without moving water when it is worn.

The page on a washing machine not draining shows what you can safely check before you call us.

Door lock and heater

The door lock on a front-loader must latch before the cycle begins. When the latch or the lock part is worn, the machine may beep, refuse to start, or keep the door shut. Please do not pry it open.

The heater warms the wash water. A failed heater can mean that cold water is used even on a hot program, or that the cycle stops early. Where the water is hard, scale on the heater is a frequent cause of trouble, so descaling and cleaning help. Do not open the heater area yourself.

Older motors: carbon brushes and bearings

Some older Beko models use a motor with carbon brushes. Brushes wear down slowly. The early signs are weak spinning, a burning smell, or sparks seen near the motor area. Stop using the machine if you notice any of these, and call us.

Bearings sit behind the drum. A rumbling sound that grows louder during spin, or a drum that feels loose when you push it by hand, usually points to worn bearings. This is a bigger repair than a filter, so the technician will explain the work before it begins. If the cost looks high against the age of the machine, read our guide to repairing or replacing.

Control module and display codes

Beko machines use an electronic control module. Faults can show as random stops, a frozen display, a cycle that will not start, or a code on the screen. Codes and their meanings depend on the model. We do not want to guess, so please check the user manual for your machine and tell us the exact code and model number.

Signs that you should stop using the machine

Some signs mean the washer should rest until someone looks at it. These include a burning smell, smoke, a tripping breaker, water around the electrical area, or a drum that scrapes while it turns. Unplug the machine, close the tap and call us.

A washer that works but sounds a little different is not an emergency. Even so, early checking is usually cheaper than waiting. A small leak or a new noise tends to grow, and clothes can be damaged along the way.
